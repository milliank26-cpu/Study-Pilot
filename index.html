How the learning algorithm works

Retrieval and feedback

Log what you can recall or apply before checking notes. Then compare with a worked solution or marking guide. Rereading can support corrective learning, but it is not counted as a successful retrieval.

Spacing

Starting intervals are a practical default: next day after first learning, then longer after independent success. Failure, hints or partial answers bring the next attempt forward. The interval is a planning heuristic, not a prediction of the exact moment you will forget.

Mastery and priority

Mastery is an estimate based on the latest recorded performance, independence and repeated success; time spent alone does not raise it. Priority combines exam relevance, importance, weakness, difficulty, overdue status, prerequisites and deadline pressure. The weights are transparent engineering choices, not a validated psychological score.

Scheduling and limits

The planner allocates work only within estimated academic capacity, reserves urgent assignment time, respects prerequisites where possible and balances new learning, review and practice. If the work does not fit, it reports a shortfall rather than promising completion. Availability estimates and learning records are self-reported, so recommendations are only as reliable as the inputs.

Research foundations

  • Dunlosky et al. (2013), effective learning techniques
  • Cepeda et al. (2006), distributed practice meta-analysis
  • Donoghue & Hattie (2021), meta-analysis of ten learning techniques
  • Institute of Education Sciences, study and instruction practice guide

Research supports the general value of practice testing and distributed practice, but ideal spacing depends on the material, retention period and learner. The app's exact weights, duration estimates and interval multipliers are practical heuristics, not direct findings from those studies. It cannot guarantee retention or examination results.
